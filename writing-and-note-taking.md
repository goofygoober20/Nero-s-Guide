## Note-Taking Systems That Actually Work {#note-systems}

Before picking a tool, understand **how** to take notes. The right system beats the fanciest app every time.

<div class="card-grid-3">
  <div class="shared-card" style="border-left: 3px solid #3498db;">
    <h4>📋 Cornell Method</h4>
    <p>Divide your page into three sections: a narrow left column for cues/questions, a wide right column for notes, and a bottom summary. After class, write questions in the left column that correspond to your notes. Cover the right side and quiz yourself.</p>
    <p><strong>Best for:</strong> Lecture-based classes, exam prep</p>
    <p><strong>Why it works:</strong> Forces active recall, the single most effective study technique.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card" style="border-left: 3px solid #9b59b6;">
    <h4>🔗 Zettelkasten (Slip-Box)</h4>
    <p>Each note is one atomic idea, linked to related notes. Instead of organizing by topic, you build a web of connections. The power grows exponentially as you add notes, ideas start finding each other.</p>
    <p><strong>Best for:</strong> Researchers, writers, long-term knowledge building</p>
    <p><strong>Why it works:</strong> Mirrors how your brain actually stores information, through association, not folders.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card" style="border-left: 3px solid #27ae60;">
    <h4>📁 PARA Method</h4>
    <p>Organize everything into four categories: <strong>P</strong>rojects (active work), <strong>A</strong>reas (ongoing responsibilities), <strong>R</strong>esources (reference material), <strong>A</strong>rchive (completed/inactive). Created by Tiago Forte.</p>
    <p><strong>Best for:</strong> Professionals, anyone juggling multiple projects</p>
    <p><strong>Why it works:</strong> Action-oriented, every note has a clear place based on how you'll use it.</p>
  </div>
</div>

::: info Other Popular Systems
- **Outline Method**: hierarchical bullet points. Simple, fast, but limited for complex ideas.
- **Mind Mapping**: visual branching from a central topic. Great for brainstorming, less useful for review.
- **SQ3R**: Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review. A reading-focused system for textbooks.
- **Mapping + Cornell Combo**: use mind maps during lectures, convert to Cornell notes for review.
:::

## Markdown: The Universal Language of Notes {#markdown}

This entire site is built with Markdown. Learning it takes 10 minutes and pays off forever. Markdown is just plain text with simple formatting syntax, it works everywhere and never becomes obsolete.

### The Essentials

```markdown
# Heading 1
## Heading 2
### Heading 3

**bold text**
*italic text*
~~strikethrough~~

- bullet list
1. numbered list
- [ ] task list (checkbox)

> blockquote

[link text](https://example.com)
![alt text](image-url.png)

`inline code`

---  (horizontal rule)
```

### Advanced Markdown

```markdown
| Column 1 | Column 2 | Column 3 |
|----------|----------|----------|
| Cell 1   | Cell 2   | Cell 3   |

<!-- comments won't render -->

Superscript: 2^10^
Subscript: H~2~O
```

::: tip Markdown is everywhere
- **GitHub** - READMEs, issues, pull requests
- **Discord/Slack** - message formatting
- **Obsidian/Logseq** - native format
- **Jupyter notebooks** - documentation cells
- **This website** - literally everything you're reading

Once you learn Markdown, you can write formatted text in almost any tool.
:::

## Building Your Second Brain {#second-brain}

A "second brain" isn't just storage, it's a thinking system. The goal: capture ideas when they happen, organize them so you can find them later, and connect them so new insights emerge.

### The Four-Step Workflow (CODE)

<div class="card-grid-2">
  <div class="shared-card" style="border-left: 3px solid #3498db;">
    <h4>1. Capture 💾</h4>
    <p>Save anything that resonates, quotes, ideas, screenshots, half-formed thoughts. Don't filter yet. The goal is to catch ideas before they evaporate. Use a quick-capture tool (Apple Notes, Google Keep, Obsidian mobile) so you never lose a thought.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card" style="border-left: 3px solid #9b59b6;">
    <h4>2. Organize 📂</h4>
    <p>Move captures into your system using PARA. Active projects get top billing. Reference material gets filed. The key question: "Is this actionable right now?" If yes → Projects. If it's a recurring responsibility → Areas. If it's useful reference → Resources.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card" style="border-left: 3px solid #27ae60;">
    <h4>3. Distill 🔗</h4>
    <p>Highlight and extract the key insights. Write brief summaries in your own words. This is where learning happens, rephrasing forces understanding. Use Zettelkasten to link ideas across different sources.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card" style="border-left: 3px solid #e67e22;">
    <h4>4. Express ✍️</h4>
    <p>Use your notes to create, write essays, build projects, make decisions. A second brain that only stores is just a graveyard. The value comes from reuse. Every note should eventually contribute to something.</p>
  </div>
</div>

::: tip Start small, grow naturally
Don't spend weeks setting up the "perfect" system. Start with 10 notes in Obsidian or Notion. Add links as you go. The system teaches itself, once you see notes connecting, you'll understand why Zettelkasten works.
:::

## AI-Assisted Writing Workflow {#ai-writing}

Inspired by [NeverWrite](https://neverwrite.app) (AI that asks before it changes.)

The future of writing isn't AI writing for you. It's AI as a thinking partner that helps you write better while keeping you in control.

### The Draft → Review → Edit Loop

<div class="card-grid-3">
  <div class="shared-card" style="border-left: 3px solid #3498db;">
    <h4>1. Write Your Draft</h4>
    <p>Get your ideas down without worrying about quality. Use a distraction-free editor (Zen, FocusWriter, or just Obsidian's write mode). The goal is flow, edit later.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card" style="border-left: 3px solid #9b59b6;">
    <h4>2. AI Reviews (Not Rewrites)</h4>
    <p>Ask AI to review your draft for clarity, structure, and flow. Use prompts like: "What's unclear here?" "Where does the argument break down?" "Suggest 3 ways to strengthen this paragraph." <strong>Never let AI rewrite your voice.</strong></p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card" style="border-left: 3px solid #27ae60;">
    <h4>3. Human Edits</h4>
    <p>You make the final decisions. Accept suggestions that resonate, reject those that don't. The writing stays yours, AI just helped you see what you missed.</p>
  </div>
</div>

### AI Writing Prompts That Help (Not Replace)

- **Clarity check:** "Review this paragraph for unclear sentences. Flag any sentence a reader might misinterpret."
- **Structure review:** "Does this essay flow logically? Suggest a better order if needed."
- **Argument strength:** "Where is my weakest claim? What evidence would strengthen it?"
- **Conciseness:** "Cut any word or sentence that doesn't earn its place."
- **Audience check:** "Would a [freshman/senior/professor] understand this? What needs simplifying?"

::: danger Never do this
- ❌ Paste your assignment and ask AI to "write it for you"
- ❌ Accept AI rewrites without reading them
- ❌ Use AI to generate citations you didn't actually read
- ❌ Let AI strip your unique voice and style

AI is a thinking tool, not a ghostwriter. Your professor wants to hear from **you**.
:::

## Academic Writing Tips {#academic-writing}

### Structure Your Papers

1. **Thesis first** - Write a one-sentence thesis before anything else. Every paragraph should support it.
2. **Outline before drafting** - Map your argument in bullet points. Rearrange until it flows.
3. **Topic sentences** - Every paragraph starts with its main claim. The rest of the paragraph supports it.
4. **Evidence sandwich** - Claim → Evidence → Analysis → Link back to thesis.
5. **Conclusion ≠ Summary** - End with implications, not repetition.

### Citation Done Right

- **Cite as you write** - Don't wait until the end. Use a citation manager from day one.
- **Citation managers:** [Zotero](https://www.zotero.org/) (free, open-source), [Mendeley](https://www.mendeley.com/), or [Paperpile](https://paperpile.com/)
- **Citation styles:** APA for psychology/social sciences, MLA for humanities, Chicago for history, IEEE for engineering.
- **When in doubt, cite it.** Over-citation is better than plagiarism.

### Avoiding Plagiarism

- **Paraphrase properly** - Read the source, close it, write the idea in your own words, then cite.
- **Quote sparingly** - Direct quotes should be rare. Use them only for precise language you can't improve.
- **Use a plagiarism checker** - [Quetext](https://www.quetext.com/), [Scribbr](https://www.scribbr.com/plagiarism-checker/), or your school's built-in tool.
- **When to cite:** Any fact, idea, or phrase that isn't common knowledge and didn't come from your own analysis.

::: tip The "So What?" Test
After every body paragraph, ask: "So what? Why does this matter to my thesis?" If you can't answer, the paragraph doesn't belong.
:::
