## Flight Planning & Booking

Finding the right flight at the right price is the first step in any trip. A few well-chosen tools can save you hours of searching and hundreds of dollars.

[**Google Flights**](https://www.google.com/travel/flights) is the best all-around flight search engine. Its date grid and price graph make it trivially easy to spot the cheapest days to fly, and price tracking sends you alerts when fares drop. It covers most airlines worldwide and its interface is fast and uncluttered.

[**Skyscanner**](https://www.skyscanner.com/) excels when you are flexible on destination. It's "everywhere" search shows you the cheapest places to fly from your airport. It also searches hotels and car rentals, making it a decent one-stop shop.

::: tip Hidden-City Ticketing
[**Skiplagged**](https://skiplagged.com/) finds "hidden city" deals where booking a flight with a layover at your actual destination is cheaper than a direct flight. This can save serious money, but read their FAQ about baggage rules first, you cannot check luggage on a skip-lagged ticket, and airlines may penalize frequent abuse.
:::

## Train & Ground Transport

For overland travel, nothing beats [**Seat 61**](https://www.seat61.com/). It is the definitive guide to train travel worldwide. Every route, every station, every booking link. If you want to travel by train anywhere on earth, start here. It also covers ferry connections when trains cross water.

[**Station View**](https://stationview.app/) shows live train departure boards for the UK, France, Switzerland, Belgium, Germany, Netherlands, Finland, and Japan. Its clean interface mirrors exactly what the station screens show, so you can check platform changes and delays without squinting at a display across a crowded concourse.

## Maps & Navigation

[**Google Maps**](https://maps.google.com/) remains the most comprehensive options, real-time traffic, public transit directions, street view, business hours and reviews, and offline maps for specific areas.

[**Apple Maps**](https://maps.apple.com/) is a privacy-focused alternative that has improved dramatically. It offers excellent turn-by-turn navigation, detailed city maps with elevation data, and offline support. [**Waze**](https://www.waze.com/) is best for daily driving in urban areas where traffic changes by the minute. It's crowdsourced police alerts and hazard reports are unmatched. [**Bing Maps**](https://www.bing.com/maps/) is a solid alternative with bird's eye aerial imagery and Ordnance Survey maps for the UK.

For true offline independence, [**OsmAnd**](https://f-droid.org/packages/net.osmand.plus/) provides OpenStreetMap-based navigation with full turn-by-turn directions, hiking trails, POIs, and topographic data, all without any internet connection. [**CoMaps**](https://comaps.app/) is a privacy-first offline maps app (a fork of Organic Maps) with no ads, no tracking, and no account required.

::: tip Offline Maps
Before traveling abroad, download offline maps for your destination in **both** Google Maps and OsmAnd. Cell service is not always reliable, and having a backup map app can save you when one service does not have a particular area well-covered.
:::

If you explore cities on two wheels, the [**Bike-Sharing World Map**](https://bikesharingworldmap.com/) shows every bike-sharing system worldwide with station locations, bike availability, and docking info.

## Accommodation

[**CozyCozy**](https://cozycozy.com/) is a metasearch for accommodation that compares websites Booking.com, Airbnb and other providers. It saves you a lot of time and I personally suggest it. 

[**Trustroots**](https://trustroots.org/) is a hospitality exchange network focused on travelers, hitchhikers, and nomads. It is completely free and community-driven, it bascially Couchsurfing for travelers, and also be safe. 

## Passport, Visa & Entry Requirements

This is the most important part of your trip. [**Entry Conditions**](https://www.entryconditions.com/) shows entry requirements, visa policies, and passport validity rules for every country. There is also a [US-specific passport page](https://www.entryconditions.com/passport/united-states) if you are an American traveler. **Check that you have your paperwork as much as you can when traveling to make sure that you dont lose it and keep it on your body to make sure that you dont get it stolen.**

[**Visa Index**](https://visaindex.com/) lets you check visa requirements by passport, which countries you can visit visa-free, visa-on-arrival, or need to apply for in advance. It is updated regularly and covers every passport.

## Weather & Environment

Weather can make or break a trip, especially if you are spending time outdoors. [**Windy**](https://www.windy.com/) is the best standard for weather visualization, its a great, interactive maps showing wind, rain, temperature, waves, and air quality layers drawn from multiple forecast models (ECMWF, GFS, NAM). [**Ventusky**](https://www.ventusky.com/) is an excellent alternative. 

For travelers heading to hurricane-prone regions, [**Tropical Tidbits**](https://www.tropicaltidbits.com/) provides advanced weather models and tropical cyclone tracking. [**XWeather**](https://www.xweather.com/) offers live weather maps with radar, satellite, and severe weather alerts in a clean interface for quick checks.

::: tip Environment & Safety
[**Flood Map**](https://floodmap.net/) shows elevation and flood risk data for any location, useful for checking whether your destination might be affected by seasonal flooding, storm surges, or rising sea levels. [**Sit in Shade**](https://sitinshade.com/) finds shaded spots along transit routes based on sun position, perfect for planning walks in hot climates.
:::

## Destination Inspiration

When you do not know where to go next, let the unusual guide you. [**Atlas Obscura**](https://www.atlasobscura.com/) is the definitive guide to the world's strangest, most wonderful places, abandoned buildings, natural oddities, unusual museums, hidden tunnels. If it is strange and worth seeing, it is here.

[**UNESCO World Heritage List**](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/) is the gold standard for culturally and naturally significant destinations worth planning a trip around. Every site is searchable by country and year. [**Roadside America**](https://www.roadsideamerica.com/) covers quirky roadside attractions across the United States, giant statues, bizarre museums, unusual architecture, perfect for road trip planning.

For practical destination guides, [**Wikivoyage**](https://en.wikivoyage.org/) is a free, up-to-date travel wiki maintained by volunteers covering transportation, safety, and sights for destinations worldwide. [**Wikitravel**](https://wikitravel.org/) is an older wiki with a lot destination guides (some content overlaps with Wikivoyage but both are worth checking for the most complete picture.)

## Cultural & Etiquette Guides

Traveling somewhere unfamiliar means navigating unfamiliar social rules. [**Cultural Atlas**](https://culturalatlas.sbs.com.au/) provides country-by-country guides to cultural norms, etiquette, communication styles, and business practices. It is invaluable for avoiding social faux pas in places where a small gesture can carry a large meaning.

## Special Interest Travel

Niche interests make for memorable trips. For theme park enthusiasts, [**RCDB**](https://www.rcdb.com/) (Roller Coaster Database) catalogs every coaster worldwide along with every stat you could want, and the [**Dark Ride Database**](https://www.darkridedatabase.com/) covers indoor tracked attractions from classic dark rides to modern trackless systems.

Aviation geeks have an embarrassment of riches. [**Flightradar24**](https://flightradar24.com/) shows live aircraft positions, routes, altitudes, and speeds. [**FlightAware**](https://www.flightaware.com/) offers more detail for actual flight planning, delays, cancellations, aircraft history, and airport conditions. [**Plane Finder**](https://planefinder.net/) is a clean alternative when the big two are overloaded.

For the most complete picture of air traffic, [**ADS-B Exchange**](https://globe.adsbexchange.com/) is community-driven and does not filter military or private aircraft — no block lists, just raw data. [**Airplanes.Live**](https://airplanes.live/) offers a modern 3D view on an open-source platform with a companion [Android app](https://github.com/d4rken-org/airplanes-live-app). [**Air Loom**](https://objectiveunclear.com/airloom.html) is more art than utility, a mesmerizing 3D visualization of live flight paths through space.

Film buffs planning trips around the best screens should bookmark [**143190.xyz**](https://143190.xyz/), an IMAX theatre database showing locations, screen types (GT, SR, Xenon, Laser), and audio systems worldwide. For October travelers, [**FrightFind**](https://frightfind.com/) finds haunted houses and Halloween events near your destination.

## Essential Tools

A few utilities that do not fit neatly into any one category but deserve a spot in every traveler's toolkit:

[**eSIMDB**](https://www.esimdb.com/) lets you compare eSIM data plans from every provider worldwide.

::: tip Connectivity
Get an eSIM **before** you travel. eSIMDB lets you compare plans so you can grab a local data plan from home without hunting for a physical SIM at your destination. Check your phone model before assuring you need it physical or digital. 
:::

## Frequently Asked Questions

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>How do I travel cheaply as a student?</summary>

Use student fares, travel in the off-season, stay in hostels or with locals, and book transport early. A student ID (ISIC) unlocks discounts on trains, museums, and sights worldwide.

</details>

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>Is travel insurance worth it?</summary>

Usually yes for international trips — a single medical or cancellation event costs far more than the premium. Check if your card or school already covers you before buying.

</details>

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>How do I stay safe abroad?</summary>

Register with your country's travel advisory service, keep digital copies of documents, share your itinerary with someone, and learn a few local phrases. Blend in and trust your instincts.

</details>

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>Do I need a passport years in advance?</summary>

Get it at least 6 months before any trip — processing can be slow and some countries require 6 months' validity beyond your stay. Renew early; don't cut it close.

</details>

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>How do I avoid getting sick while traveling?</summary>

Drink bottled or filtered water where unsafe, wash hands often, get recommended vaccines, and ease into new foods. Travel insurance that covers illness is your backup.

</details>

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>What's the best way to find cheap flights?</summary>

Search in private/incognito mode, compare across a few aggregators, and be flexible on dates and nearby airports. Set price alerts and book 1-3 months out for the best balance.

</details>
