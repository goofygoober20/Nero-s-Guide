## The Pomodoro Technique

<div class="shared-card reveal">
  <p>The Pomodoro Technique is simple: work for <strong>25 minutes</strong>, take a <strong>5-minute break</strong>. After four pomodoros, take a longer break (15-30 minutes). This cycle helps maintain focus and prevents burnout.</p>
</div>

<div class="steps-grid">
  <div class="step-card reveal">
    <span class="step-number">1</span>
    <div>
      <strong>Pick a Task</strong>
      <p>Choose one thing to work on. No multitasking - commit to this single task for one pomodoro.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="step-card reveal">
    <span class="step-number">2</span>
    <div>
      <strong>Set a Timer</strong>
      <p>25 minutes. Close distractions. Use a physical timer or one of the apps listed below.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="step-card reveal">
    <span class="step-number">3</span>
    <div>
      <strong>Work With Focus</strong>
      <p>No interruptions. If something comes up, write it down and deal with it after the timer ends.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
  <div class="step-card reveal">
    <span class="step-number">4</span>
    <div>
      <strong>Take a Break</strong>
      <p>Step away from your desk. Stretch, walk, get water. Let your brain reset before the next round.</p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

## Task Management

<div class="card-grid-2">
  <div class="shared-card reveal">
    <h4>GTD (Getting Things Done)</h4>
    <p>Capture everything in an inbox, clarify what it means, organize by context, review weekly, and do. The gold standard methodology.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card reveal">
    <h4>Kanban Boards</h4>
    <p>Visualize your workflow: To Do, Doing, Done. Limit work in progress. Tools: Trello, GitHub Projects, Notion, Todoist.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card reveal">
    <h4>Eisenhower Matrix</h4>
    <p>Quadrant-based: Urgent/Important, Not Urgent/Important, Urgent/Not Important, Not Urgent/Not Important. Prioritize accordingly.</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card reveal">
    <h4>Time Blocking</h4>
    <p>Schedule every part of your day in blocks. Put deep work first (morning), meetings in the afternoon. Google Calendar or any calendar app.</p>
  </div>
</div>

### The Ivy Lee Method

A simple but powerful daily planning technique:

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | At the end of each day, write down the **6 most important tasks** for tomorrow |
| 2 | **Prioritize them** in order of importance |
| 3 | The next morning, work on **task 1** until it's complete |
| 4 | Then move to **task 2**, and so on |
| 5 | **Move unfinished tasks** to the next day's list |

> **Why it works:** It forces focus on what actually matters, not just what's urgent or easy. Six tasks is realistic - any more and you're probably overcommitting.

## Frequently Asked Questions

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>What's the best productivity system?</summary>

The one you'll actually use. Start simple: a short to-do list plus a calendar. Add structure (time-blocking, GTD, Pomodoro) only when a specific problem appears.

</details>

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>How do I stop procrastinating?</summary>

Shrink the task until it's too small to fear — open the doc, write one sentence. Action creates momentum; motivation usually follows, not leads.

</details>

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>Should I use a to-do list or a calendar?</summary>

Both, for different things. Calendars hold time-bound commitments; to-do lists hold tasks. Put only the day's top 3 on the list so it stays actionable.

</details>

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>How do I focus with so many distractions?</summary>

Remove them physically: phone in another room, one tab open, notifications off. Attention is a muscle — protect it like you would a deadline.

</details>

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>Is multitasking real?</summary>

Not for thinking work. The brain switches between tasks and loses time each switch. Batch similar work and do one thing at a time for deep tasks.

</details>

<details class="faq-item">
<summary>What do I do when I'm burned out?</summary>

Rest without guilt — burnout isn't fixed by pushing harder. Sleep, move, and cut optional commitments for a few days. Recovery is productive, not lazy.

</details>
