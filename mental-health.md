This is one of the most practical self-care tools on the internet. It's designed for people who struggle with executive dysfunction or have trouble reading internal signals. The guide walks you through simple yes/no questions about basic needs:

## 🧠 Understanding Your Mood {#understanding-mood}

> **Source:** Based on Dr. Julie Smith's *Why Has Nobody Told Me This Before?*

### How Feelings Actually Work

| Key Idea | Explanation |
|----------|-------------|
| **Low mood happens to everyone** | We all have down days. It's not a "brain problem" — it's human. |
| **Your mood can change** | Like body temperature, mood is affected by sleep, food, stress, and environment. |
| **You can influence your mood** | Science shows we have more control than we think — through actions, thoughts, and body care. |
| **Build your mental toolkit** | We can't eliminate low moods (life is hard!), but we can learn skills to handle them better. |

### Thoughts, Feelings, Body, Actions — They're All Connected

| Component | How It Affects You |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Thoughts** | Affect feelings AND are affected by feelings |
| **Feelings** | Your brain's best guess based on body signals, memories, and context |
| **Body sensations** | Tiredness, hunger, pain all send signals your brain interprets as mood |
| **Actions** | Doing less → feeling lower → doing even less (a negative cycle) |

### Journaling Prompts to Understand Your Patterns

When you feel low, ask yourself:

- What happened right before?
- What thoughts came up?
- What did I focus on?
- What feelings were there?
- How did my body feel?
- What did I feel like doing?
- What did I actually do?
- How did my actions affect my feelings?

## 🔄 Common Thinking Traps {#thinking-traps}

These are normal, but they make low moods worse if you believe them without question.

| Thinking Trap | What It Sounds Like | How to Break It |
|---------------|---------------------|-----------------|
| **Guessing what others think** | "They think I'm stupid" | Ask for proof. You don't actually know. |
| **One bad thing = everything is ruined** | "This always happens to me" | Look for counterexamples. |
| **Believing feelings are facts** | "I feel useless, so I must be useless" | Feelings are interpretations, not truth. |
| **Focusing only on negatives** | Ignoring any positive feedback | Actively look for what went well. |
| **"Should" and "must" rules** | "I should never fail" | Replace with "It would be nice if..." |
| **Black-or-white thinking** | "I'm either a success or a failure" | Find the gray area. |

### What to Do About Thinking Traps

| Strategy | How to Use It |
|----------|---------------|
| **Notice them** | Awareness is the first step. Start by journaling after the fact. |
| **Use distancing language** | Say "I'm having the thought that..." instead of "I am..." |
| **Name the trap** | Giving it a name ("There's catastrophizing again") reduces its power. |
| **Ask a trusted friend** | They can spot patterns you miss. |
| **Practice mindfulness** | Observe thoughts without getting caught in them. |

## 🛠️ Practical Tools & Techniques {#tools}

### Grounding Exercises {#grounding}

Use these when you feel anxious, triggered, or disconnected from reality:

| Technique | How to Do It |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Deep breathing** | Slow, calm breaths — exhale longer than you inhale |
| **5-4-3-2-1** | Name 5 things you see, 4 you feel, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste |
| **State the facts** | Say out loud: your name, age, date, location, what you've done today |
| **Splash cold water** | On your face or run hands under the faucet |
| **Body scan** | Pay close attention to each body part one by one |
| **Make tea** | Feel the warmth in your hands, taste it slowly |
| **Categories game** | Name types of dogs, gemstones, countries — anything |
| **Squiggle** | Wiggle, dance, stretch — be silly and active for a few minutes |

### Mindfulness & Gratitude {#mindfulness}

| Practice | How To | Why It Works |
|----------|--------|--------------|
| **Mindful observation** | Watch thoughts come and go without judging them | Creates space between you and your thoughts |
| **Gratitude journaling** | Write 3 things you're thankful for daily (big or small) | Trains your brain to notice positives |
| **"I get to..."** | Replace "I have to..." with "I get to..." | Shifts perspective from burden to opportunity |

### The Self-Soothing Box {#self-soothing}

Prepare a box ahead of time (when you're calm) with things that help you feel safe:

| Category | Ideas |
|----------|-------|
| **Sight** | Photos of loved ones, calming images |
| **Sound** | A playlist of comforting music |
| **Smell** | Essential oil (lavender), a familiar scent |
| **Touch** | Soft blanket, stress ball, favorite fabric |
| **Taste** | Herbal tea bag, hard candy |
| **Connection** | A note reminding you to call someone |

Keep it handy for tough moments.

## 💪 The Basics: Sleep, Food, Movement, Connection {#basics}

> These are your **mental health defense team**. When you feel bad, check these first.

### Exercise

| Why It Works | How to Start |
|--------------|--------------|
| Releases chemicals that increase your ability to enjoy things | Find movement you actually like |
| Helps medication work better | Start with 5-10 minutes |
| Being active in nature is extra helpful | Yoga counts! |
| Focus on how moving *feels*, not how it looks | Don't worry about intensity |

### Sleep

| The Cycle | What to Do |
|-----------|------------|
| Poor sleep → worse mental health → even poorer sleep | Prioritize regular sleep/wake times |
| | Get sunlight in the morning |
| | Dim lights and put screens away at night |
| | Keep a notepad for worries so you can set them aside |

### Nutrition

| Fact | Action |
|------|--------|
| Your brain and body are connected — food affects mood | Eat whole, unprocessed foods when possible |
| Healthy diets help with depression symptoms | Don't aim for perfection — one small change at a time |

### Routine & Connection

| Need | Why It Matters |
|------|----------------|
| **Routine** | Structure helps us feel safe. Small daily habits add up. |
| **Connection** | Good relationships are powerful protectors of mental health. Fight the urge to isolate — connect *before* you feel like it. |

> **Key insight:** You don't need deep conversations. Just being around others helps.

## 🎯 Building Confidence & Fighting Imposter Syndrome {#confidence}

### The Truth About Confidence

| Common Myth | Reality |
|--------------|---------|
| "Confidence is something you're born with" | Confidence is built, not inherited. |
| "I need to feel confident before I try" | Courage comes first. Confidence grows *outside* your comfort zone. |
| "Successful people don't feel like imposters" | High achievers feel imposter syndrome most strongly. |

### The Confidence-Building Recipe

| Step | What to Do |
|------|------------|
| **1. Put in the reps** | Practice the necessary skills consistently |
| **2. Stress test** | Add pressure gradually (time limit, observers) |
| **3. Examine performance** | What worked? What needs improvement? |
| **4. Turn insights into action** | Practice areas that need work. Repeat. |

### Dealing with Imposter Thoughts

| Strategy | Example |
|----------|---------|
| **Spot the bias** | Notice when you're only seeing incompetence or imagined judgment |
| **Be honest** | "I'm a beginner at this" — most people respect honesty |
| **Self-respect over self-esteem** | "I'm learning, and that's okay" |
| **Make fair comparisons** | Compare yourself to where you were last month, not to experts |
| **Check expectations** | Are your expectations realistic for your current stage? |

## 💬 Supporting Others (Friends & Family) {#supporting-others}

### What Helps — And What Doesn't

| Do This | Avoid This |
|---------|------------|
| ✅ Just be there. Your presence matters. | ❌ Trying to "fix" them or give unsolicited advice |
| ✅ Ask how they'd like to be supported | ❌ Saying "just think positive" or "snap out of it" |
| ✅ Listen without judgment | ❌ Making it about you |
| ✅ Offer practical help (meals, chores) | ❌ Expecting linear progress — healing has ups and downs |
| ✅ Take care of yourself too (you can't pour from an empty cup) | ❌ Burning out by giving too much |

### What to Say

| Instead of... | Try... |
|---------------|--------|
| "Are you okay?" (easy to say "fine") | "What's on your mind?" |
| "You shouldn't feel that way" | "That sounds really hard. I'm here." |
| "I know exactly how you feel" | "I can't fully know, but I want to understand." |
| "Have you tried...?" | "What's helped in the past?" (if they want advice) |

### When to Get Professional Help

If someone talks about hopelessness, not wanting to wake up, or you fear for their safety:
- Stay with them if possible
- Encourage them to call a crisis line
- Help them contact a mental health professional

## ⚰️ Grief & Loss {#grief}

Grief isn't just about death — you can grieve after any significant ending or loss (job loss, relationship ending, major life change).

### Common Grief Experiences (Not Stages — They Don't Happen in Order)

| Experience | What It Feels Like | What Helps |
|------------|--------------------|-------------|
| **Denial/Shock** | Numb, can't believe it's real | This protects you initially; it fades naturally |
| **Anger** | Often covers deep pain or fear | Safe release (exercise, journaling) |
| **Bargaining** | "What if..." or "If only..." | Recognize this as your brain trying to regain control |
| **Deep sadness** | Feeling empty, heavy | Comfort and support, not being told to "snap out of it" |
| **Acceptance** | Not liking the loss, but learning to live with the new reality | Finding ways to stay connected to what was lost while still living |

### The Dual Process Model

Grief involves moving back and forth between:

| Focus on Loss | Focus on Restoration |
|---------------|----------------------|
| Feeling the pain | Dealing with life changes |
| Remembering | Doing new things |
| Crying | Taking breaks, distracting yourself |

> **You need both.** You need to feel the pain AND you need breaks to rest. Neither is wrong.

### Finding Strength in Grief (Julia Samuel's Pillars)

| Pillar | What to Focus On |
|--------|------------------|
| **Relationship with what was lost** | Find new ways to feel connected (visiting special places, rituals) |
| **Relationship with yourself** | Pay attention to your own needs and well-being |
| **Expressing grief** | Talk, write, create art — let it out |
| **Time** | Be patient. There's no set timeline. |
| **Mind & body connection** | Exercise, sleep, food, connection with supportive people |
| **Setting limits** | It's okay to set boundaries around others' expectations |
