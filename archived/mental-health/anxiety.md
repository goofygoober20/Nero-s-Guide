# Anxiety & Overthinking

## Facing Anxiety

Anxiety feels bad, so naturally we want it gone. But your body's anxiety response is an alarm system designed to keep you safe. Like a smoke alarm, it can go off for "burnt toast" - perceived threats - not just real fires. Fear is the signal, not the problem itself. Judging or fighting fear keeps you stuck.

Trying to escape the feeling quickly - avoiding situations, using distractions - gives temporary relief but reinforces the fear long-term. Your brain learns "avoidance equals safety," strengthening the anxiety cycle. The real problem becomes avoidance itself, leading you to shrink your life to avoid triggers.

The goal isn't to eliminate anxiety. You can't remove your body's ability to feel fear. Instead, the goal is willingness - being willing to experience it while acting on what matters. Acknowledge that fear is temporary and use it as fuel.

Fierce compassion means facing fear with determination and commitment to having your own back. Talk to yourself supportively but firmly, like a warrior driving forward through fear for what's right.

See fear not as weakness, but as a sign you're facing something important. It's the core ingredient of courage.

#### Section Summary

- Wanting anxiety gone is normal, but trying to eliminate it often makes it worse.
- Anxiety is your body's safety alarm. Avoidance reinforces anxiety long-term.
- Goal: Willingness to feel fear while living your life. Face fears gradually with coping skills, fierce self-compassion, and reframing.

## Things That Make Anxiety Worse

Avoidance is the main culprit. Avoiding situations or feelings that trigger anxiety stops your brain from learning that you can cope. This keeps anxiety going.

Safety actions that keep anxiety alive:
- **Escape.** Leaving a feared place before the anxiety naturally calms down.
- **Anxious avoidance.** Saying no to things, not going places, putting things off.
- **Doing extra things afterwards.** Excessive cleaning or checking to feel safe.
- **Worrying ahead.** Constantly thinking about everything that could go wrong.
- **Seeking reassurance.** Constantly asking others if things will be okay.
- **Relying on crutches.** Feeling you can only cope with certain things or people present.
- **Subtle avoidance.** Using your phone, going to the bathroom often, leaving early to avoid awkwardness.

#### Section Summary

- Avoiding things that make you anxious is the main way anxiety stays strong.
- Things you do to feel safer in the moment actually keep the anxiety going long-term because you don't learn you can handle it.

## Calming Anxiety Quickly

Anxiety often involves fast, shallow breaths. Slowing your breathing calms your body. Breathing slowly, especially making the out-breath longer and stronger than the in-breath, helps slow your heart rate and reduces the physical feelings of anxiety.

#### Section Summary

- To calm anxiety quickly, control your breathing: slow it down, make the out-breath longer.
- Practice techniques like box breathing regularly.
- Use physical movement to release anxious energy.

## Dealing with Anxious Thoughts & Overthinking

Your brain pays attention to possible dangers, which is why scary thoughts about worst-case scenarios are hard to ignore. Overthinking often increases when overall stress is high - poor sleep, caffeine, life pressures. Address the basics first: sleep, nutrition, exercise.

### Get Distance from Your Thoughts

Anxious thoughts are just guesses, stories, and biased interpretations. Believing them gives them power. To step back:

- Practice mindfulness - watch thoughts without getting hooked.
- Notice and name thinking traps (below).
- Use distancing language: "I notice I'm having the thought that..."
- Write thoughts down to see them clearly and reduce their status from facts to ideas.

### Spot Thinking Traps

Common traps with worry and overthinking:
- **Catastrophizing.** Imagining the absolute worst outcome.
- **Black-and-white thinking.** Seeing things in extremes.
- **Fortune-telling.** Assuming negative predictions are true just because you thought them.
- Also common: personalizing, mental filter, overgeneralizing, labeling.

Once you spot a bias, get curious. Ask: Is it valid? Likely? Important? Changeable? Where's the bias? Is this thought helpful?

### Choose Your Focus

You control where you aim your attention, even if anxious thoughts are still around. Focusing on worries fuels anxiety. Shifting focus reduces their impact. The antidote to destructive overthinking is action. Identify which worries are true, important, and changeable. Make a plan for those. Take one small step.

What to focus on instead:
- **Kind self-talk.** Counter scary thoughts with calm, reassuring, encouraging words.
- **Reframing.** See situations as challenges. Focus on preparation and positive action.
- **Your values and identity.** Base actions on what matters, not fear.

#### Section Summary

- Overthinking is often linked to overall stress levels - address the basics first.
- Step back from anxious thoughts. Question their validity, likelihood, importance, and helpfulness.
- Take command of your attention. Shift focus from unhelpful worries to constructive action.

## Facing Fears About Life Ending

Fear of dying is a deep and common human fear. It can fuel other anxieties like health worries, panic, and phobias. We cope by trying to feel safe - avoiding risks, seeking lasting impact, or focusing on connections.

Paradoxically, thinking about death can give life more meaning and help us live more intentionally. It makes us ask what truly matters.

Unhelpful beliefs, like "death is unfair" or "it will be unbearable pain," make the natural fear much worse. Avoidance doesn't work - trying to feel completely safe from death leads to avoiding more and more in life.

Acceptance is key. This doesn't mean liking or wanting death. It means stopping the fight against the reality that life ends and we don't control everything. Acceptance frees us to live more fully now.

If you could write a short message on your gravestone, what would you want it to say about how you lived? This kind of question helps clarify your values and how you want to live now, because life is limited.

#### Section Summary

- Fear of death is common and can drive other anxieties.
- Facing it can help create a more meaningful life.
- Avoidance doesn't work long-term. Acceptance is key.
- Explore your beliefs, values, and desired legacy to find meaning now.
