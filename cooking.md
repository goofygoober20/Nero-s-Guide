## 🚀 Quick Start {#quick-start}

**TL;DR:** Start with simple recipes (3 steps or less), master a few staples, and cook more than you need so leftovers become next week's lunch.

### The Beginner's Mindset

| Don't | Do |
|-------|-----|
| ❌ Start with complicated multi-course meals | ✅ Master one simple dish at a time |
| ❌ Buy every gadget and tool | ✅ Build your kitchen gradually |
| ❌ Compare yourself to TV chefs | ✅ Celebrate small wins (edible is success!) |
| ❌ Give up after one failure | ✅ Learn what went wrong and try again |

> Every expert cook was once a beginner who burned toast. Keep going. [citation:1]

## 📖 Cooking Terms Every Home Cook Should Know {#terms}

Understanding recipe language removes the intimidation factor. Here's what those words actually mean. [citation:6]

### Knife Cuts (Size Matters)

| Term | Size | Looks Like | Used For |
|------|------|------------|----------|
| **Chop** | ~½ inch pieces | Rough, uneven squares | Onions, bell peppers, carrots |
| **Dice** | ⅛-½ inch cubes | Small, even squares | Even cooking in soups/stews |
| **Mince** | As small as possible | Tiny bits | Garlic, ginger, fresh herbs |
| **Slice** | Thin, flat pieces | Coins or planks | Potatoes for au gratin, onions for caramelizing |

> *"Finely chop"* = make pieces super small. *"Roughly chop"* = leave them larger. [citation:6]

### Measurements Without Measuring Spoons

| Term | Approximate amount |
|------|-------------------|
| **Dash** | ⅛ teaspoon |
| **Pinch** | ⅛ teaspoon (what you can pinch between fingers) |
| **Smidgen** | ⅛ teaspoon |
| **Season to taste** | Add salt/pepper gradually, taste as you go |

### Dry-Heat Cooking (Oven & Stovetop)

| Term | What Happens | Best For |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Bake** | Surround food with hot air (savory) | Casseroles, roasted vegetables |
| **Roast** | Same as bake, but higher temp | Meat, potatoes, root veg |
| **Broil** | High heat from top only | Browning tops, melting cheese |
| **Sauté** | Quick cook over high heat, stirring | Vegetables, thin chicken cutlets |
| **Sear** | Brown briefly over high heat, no stirring | Steaks, chops (seals in flavor) |
| **Pan fry** | Cook in ~½ inch oil | Chicken cutlets, fish |
| **Deep fry** | Fully submerge in hot oil | French fries, doughnuts |

### Moist-Heat Cooking (Water & Steam)

| Term | Temperature | Movement | Best For |
|------|-------------|----------|----------|
| **Poach** | 160-180°F (gentle) | No bubbles | Eggs, delicate fish |
| **Simmer** | 180-205°F (just below boil) | Small bubbles | Soups, sauces, beans |
| **Boil** | 212°F (full boil) | Rolling bubbles | Pasta, potatoes, hard-boiled eggs |
| **Steam** | Food above boiling water | No direct water contact | Broccoli, fish, dumplings |
| **Blanch** | Boil briefly, then ice bath | 1-5 minutes | Bright green veggies, freezing prep |

### The Combo Method: Braising

> **Braising** = sear first (dry heat) + cook low and slow in liquid (moist heat). Perfect for tough cuts of meat that become fall-apart tender. Stew is the same thing, just with smaller pieces of meat. [citation:6]

## 🍚 Mastering Staples (Rice, Beans, Eggs) {#staples}

Master these three cheap, versatile ingredients, and you'll never go hungry.

### Perfect Rice Every Time (Stovetop Method)

The number one mistake people make is stirring the rice while it cooks. **Never stir rice** — it releases starch and makes it sticky. [citation:10]

**Basmati or Long-Grain White Rice:**

| Step | What to Do |
|------|------------|
| 1 | Rinse rice in cold water until water runs clear (removes excess starch) [citation:10] |
| 2 | Optional but worth it: Soak for 20-30 minutes |
| 3 | Combine 1 cup rice + 2 cups water in a pot |
| 4 | Bring to a boil, then reduce heat to lowest setting |
| 5 | Cover with tight-fitting lid, simmer 10-12 minutes |
| 6 | Turn off heat, leave covered 5-10 minutes |
| 7 | Fluff with a fork (never a spoon — forks separate grains) |

**Brown Rice:** Use 1 cup rice + 2 cups water. Simmer 30-35 minutes, then let rest covered for 10 minutes. [citation:3]

> **Pro tip:** Cooked rice should be eaten within 24 hours or frozen. Cool it within 1 hour of cooking and refrigerate. Never leave rice at room temperature. [citation:3]

### Beans From Scratch (Cheaper & Better Than Canned)

Canned beans are convenient, but dried beans cost pennies and let you control salt and additives. [citation:4]

**Basic Pinto or Black Beans (Stovetop):**

| Step | What to Do |
|------|------------|
| 1 | Soak dried beans overnight (6-8 hours) in cool water |
| 2 | Drain and rinse |
| 3 | In a large pot, sauté onion and garlic in oil until soft |
| 4 | Add beans, cover with water by 2 inches |
| 5 | Simmer 40-50 minutes until tender |
| 6 | Season at the end (salt toughens beans if added too early — wait until they're soft) [citation:4] |

**Instant Pot Method (No Soaking Required):**

For Great Northern or white beans: Add 1 cup beans + 3½ cups water to Instant Pot. Pressure cook on high for 27-32 minutes. Natural release for 15 minutes. [citation:8]

> **Storage:** Cooked beans keep 5 days in the fridge or 1 month in the freezer. Make a big batch and portion out. [citation:4]

### Eggs: The Ultimate Beginner Protein

Eggs teach you heat control. Start here.

| Method | Difficulty | Key Tip |
|--------|------------|---------|
| **Scrambled** | Easy | Low heat, constant stirring, remove from heat before they look done |
| **Fried (sunny side up)** | Easy | Medium heat, cover pan to cook the top |
| **Hard-boiled** | Easy | Boil 9-10 minutes, then ice bath immediately |
| **Omelette** | Medium | Fillings go on one half, fold, cover to melt cheese |
| **Poached** | Harder | Fresh eggs only, vinegar in water, gentle swirl |

## 📦 Batch Cooking & Meal Prep {#batch}

> **Batch cooking** = make large quantities of food once, eat for several days, freeze the rest. Saves time and money. [citation:7]

### What Freezes Well

| Freezes Great | Avoid Freezing |
|---------------|----------------|
| Chili, soups, stews | Cream-based sauces |
| Cooked beans, rice | Raw potatoes |
| Meatballs, cooked chicken | Fried foods (get soggy) |
| Tomato sauce, curry | Cooked pasta (reheats mushy) |
| Burritos, breakfast sandwiches | Delicate vegetables |

### One-Ingredient, Infinite Meals

Master one basic recipe, then adapt it based on what's in your fridge. [citation:7]

**Basic Curry Formula:**
1. Sauté onion + garlic
2. Add curry paste/powder + canned tomatoes
3. Add any protein + any vegetables
4. Simmer until cooked
5. Serve over rice

**Fridge-Raid Fried Rice Formula:**
1. Cook leftover rice (day-old is best)
2. Scramble an egg, set aside
3. Sauté any vegetables + leftover meat
4. Add rice + soy sauce + egg
5. Stir-fry until hot

### How to Store Cooked Food Safely

| Food | Fridge | Freezer |
|------|--------|---------|
| Cooked rice | 1 day (eat quickly!) | 1 month |
| Beans | 5 days | 1 month |
| Soup/stew | 4 days | 3 months |
| Cooked meat | 4 days | 3 months |
| Curry | 4 days | 3 months |

### What to Always Have in Your Cupboard

These staples cost little, last forever, and form the backbone of countless meals. [citation:7]

| Pantry Item | Used For |
|-------------|----------|
| **Canned tomatoes** | Pasta sauce, soup, curry base |
| **Canned beans (or dried)** | Chili, tacos, salads, rice bowls |

## 🍰 Baking Basics {#baking}

Baking is more precise than cooking (it's chemistry). But once you understand the rules, it's very forgiving within them.

### Double Pie Crust Method

For fruit pies that slice cleanly (no soupy mess): [citation:9]

| Step | What to Do |
|------|------------|
| 1 | Roll bottom crust, trim to ½ inch beyond pan edge |
| 2 | Add filling |
| 3 | Roll top crust to 12" round, place over filling |
| 4 | Fold top crust under bottom crust edge, crimp with fingers or fork |
| 5 | Chill 20-30 minutes (critical for flakiness — keeps fat cold) |
| 6 | Cut vents in top crust (steam needs to escape) |
| 7 | Bake at 425°F for 15-20 minutes, then reduce to 350°F |
| 8 | Bake until filling bubbles through vents (use a baking sheet under to catch drips) |

> **Why high heat first?** The initial high temperature melts fat and creates steam rapidly, which gives you flaky layers. Lower heat finishes cooking without burning. [citation:9]

### Simple Swaps for Plant-Based Baking

- **Instead of butter:** Coconut oil or plant-based butter sticks
- **Instead of milk:** Oat, almond, or soy milk
- **Instead of eggs:** Flax egg (1 tbsp ground flax + 3 tbsp water, let sit 5 min)
