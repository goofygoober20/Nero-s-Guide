## Cooking Terms Every Home Cook Should Know {#terms}

### Knife Cuts (Size Matters)

<div class="card-grid-2">
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>Chop</h4>
    <p>~½ inch pieces, rough uneven squares. Used for onions, bell peppers, carrots</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>Dice</h4>
    <p>⅛-½ inch cubes, small even squares. Used for even cooking in soups/stews</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>Mince</h4>
    <p>As small as possible, tiny bits. Used for garlic, ginger, fresh herbs</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>Slice</h4>
    <p>Thin flat pieces (coins or planks). Used for potatoes, onions for caramelizing</p>
  </div>
</div>

### Dry-Heat Cooking (Oven & Stovetop)

<div class="grid" style="display: grid; grid-template-columns: repeat(auto-fill, minmax(260px, 1fr)); gap: 0.75rem; margin: 1rem 0;">
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>Bake</h4>
    <p>Surround food with hot air. Best for casseroles, roasted vegetables</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>Roast</h4>
    <p>Same as bake but higher temp. Best for meat, potatoes, root veg</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>Broil</h4>
    <p>High heat from top only. Best for browning tops, melting cheese</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>Sauté</h4>
    <p>Quick cook over high heat, stirring. Best for vegetables, thin cutlets</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>Sear</h4>
    <p>Brown briefly over high heat, no stirring. Best for steaks, chops</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>Pan Fry</h4>
    <p>Cook in ~½ inch oil. Best for chicken cutlets, fish</p>
  </div>
</div>

### Moist-Heat Cooking (Water & Steam)

<div class="grid" style="display: grid; grid-template-columns: repeat(auto-fill, minmax(260px, 1fr)); gap: 0.75rem; margin: 1rem 0;">
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>Poach</h4>
    <p>160-180°F (gentle, no bubbles). Best for eggs, delicate fish</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>Simmer</h4>
    <p>180-205°F (just below boil, small bubbles). Best for soups, sauces, beans</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>Boil</h4>
    <p>212°F (full boil, rolling bubbles). Best for pasta, potatoes, hard-boiled eggs</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>Steam</h4>
    <p>Food above boiling water. Best for broccoli, fish, dumplings</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>Blanch</h4>
    <p>Boil briefly then ice bath (1-5 min). Best for bright green veggies, freezing prep</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="section-card">
  <strong>Braising:</strong> Sear first (dry heat) + cook low and slow in liquid (moist heat). Perfect for tough cuts of meat that become fall-apart tender. Stew is the same thing, just with smaller pieces of meat.
</div>

## Mastering Staples (Rice, Beans, Eggs) {#staples}

### Perfect Rice Every Time (Stovetop Method)

The number one mistake people make is stirring the rice while it cooks. <strong>Never stir rice</strong> — it releases starch and makes it sticky.

<div class="card-grid-2">
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>1. Rinse</h4>
    <p>Rinse rice in cold water until water runs clear (removes excess starch)</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>2. Soak (Optional)</h4>
    <p>Soak for 20-30 minutes for fluffier results</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>3. Combine</h4>
    <p>1 cup rice + 2 cups water in a pot</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>4. Boil then Simmer</h4>
    <p>Bring to a boil, reduce to lowest setting, cover tightly, simmer 10-12 minutes</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>5. Rest</h4>
    <p>Turn off heat, leave covered 5-10 minutes</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>6. Fluff</h4>
    <p>Fluff with a fork (never a spoon — forks separate grains)</p>
  </div>
</div>

> **Pro tip:** Cooked rice should be eaten within 24 hours or frozen. Cool it within 1 hour of cooking and refrigerate. Never leave rice at room temperature.

### Beans From Scratch (Cheaper & Better Than Canned)

<div class="card-grid-2">
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>1. Soak</h4>
    <p>Soak dried beans overnight (6-8 hours) in cool water</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>2. Drain &amp; Rinse</h4>
    <p>Drain and rinse the soaked beans</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>3. Sauté Aromatics</h4>
    <p>In a large pot, sauté onion and garlic in oil until soft</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>4. Simmer</h4>
    <p>Add beans, cover with water by 2 inches. Simmer 40-50 minutes until tender</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>5. Season at the End</h4>
    <p>Salt toughens beans if added too early — wait until they're soft</p>
  </div>
</div>

> **Storage:** Cooked beans keep 5 days in the fridge or 1 month in the freezer. Make a big batch and portion out.

### Eggs: The Ultimate Beginner Protein

<div class="card-grid-2">
  <div class="shared-card" style="border-left: 3px solid #27ae60;">

## Baking Basics {#baking}

### Double Pie Crust Method

<div class="card-grid-2">
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>1. Bottom Crust</h4>
    <p>Roll bottom crust, trim to ½ inch beyond pan edge</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>2. Add Filling</h4>
    <p>Add your fruit or savory filling</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>3. Top Crust</h4>
    <p>Roll top crust to 12" round, place over filling</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>4. Crimp</h4>
    <p>Fold top crust under bottom crust edge, crimp with fingers or fork</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>5. Chill</h4>
    <p>Chill 20-30 minutes (critical for flakiness — keeps fat cold)</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>6. Vent</h4>
    <p>Cut vents in top crust (steam needs to escape)</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>7. Bake</h4>
    <p>Bake at 425°F for 15-20 min, then reduce to 350°F</p>
  </div>
  <div class="shared-card">
    <h4>8. Check</h4>
    <p>Bake until filling bubbles through vents (use baking sheet to catch drips)</p>
  </div>
</div>
