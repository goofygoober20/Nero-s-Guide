## What is Insomnia?

Insomnia is the persistent difficulty falling asleep, staying asleep, or waking too early despite having the opportunity to sleep. It affects roughly **1 in 3 adults** at some point in their lives and can significantly impact daytime energy, mood, and health.

| Type | Duration | Description |
|---|---|---|
| **Acute** | Days to weeks | Short-term, triggered by stress, travel, or life events |
| **Chronic** | 3+ months | Happens at least 3 nights per week, often requires structured treatment |
| **Onset** | Per night | Trouble falling asleep at the beginning of the night |
| **Maintenance** | Per night | Waking frequently or too early, unable to return to sleep |

## Strategies That Actually Work

### Sleep Hygiene
- Keep the same **wake time** every day, including weekends
- No **caffeine** after 2 PM
- No **alcohol** within 3 hours of bed
- Bedroom at **65-68°F** (18-20°C), dark, and quiet
- No **screens** 30-60 minutes before bed
- Use **blackout curtains** or a sleep mask
- Get **morning sunlight** within 30 minutes of waking to anchor your circadian rhythm

### Stimulus Control
- Only go to bed when you're actually **sleepy**, not just tired
- If you can't sleep after **20 minutes**, get up and do something calm in dim light
- Use the bed **only for sleep**, no working, eating, or scrolling
- This retrains the brain to associate the bed with sleep rather than frustration

### Cognitive Approaches
- **Paradoxical intention**: Try to stay awake instead of forcing sleep, removes performance anxiety
- **Cognitive restructuring**: Identify and challenge unhelpful thoughts like "I'll never get to sleep" or "I'll be useless tomorrow"
- **Mindfulness**: Notice thoughts about sleep without engaging with them, let them pass like clouds

### Wind-Down Routine
A consistent 20-30 minute pre-bed ritual signals to the body that sleep is coming:
- Dim the lights and put devices away
- Read a physical book (not a screen)
- Light stretching or gentle yoga
- Journaling to offload racing thoughts
- Warm shower or bath (the temperature drop afterward promotes sleep)
- Herbal tea (chamomile, valerian root)

## Guided Relaxation Audio

If you're tired but can't sleep, or only have 20 minutes for a reset, these guided sessions work well:

| Session | Length | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| **Huberman Lab NSDR** | ~10 min | Quick daytime reset, falling back asleep after waking |
| **Ally Boothroyd Yoga Nidra** | ~20 min | Deep relaxation, full wind-down, replacing a partial sleep cycle |

> **NSDR** (Non-Sleep Deep Rest) guides the brain into a deeply restorative state without requiring full sleep. **Yoga Nidra** is an ancient guided relaxation practice that produces similar theta brainwave activity to sleep. Both are available free on YouTube.

## When to Seek Professional Help

  <span>If insomnia consistently affects your daytime functioning, mood, or health for <strong>more than 4 weeks</strong>, consider seeing a doctor or sleep specialist. <strong>CBT-I</strong> (Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia) is the most effective long-term treatment, it addresses the thoughts and behaviors that maintain insomnia without relying on medication.</span>
